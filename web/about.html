Founded by Humla's youth, for Humla

Rural Empowerment and Development Initiative (ग्रामीण सशक्तिकरण तथा विकास पहल) is a volunteer-led Nepali NGO, started in 2008 by a group of friends who believed in helping others change their lives.

Simikot Valley · photo: Mohan K. Duwal, CC BY-SA 4.0
Mission

Empowering people to transform their lives

We work toward a self-reliant, prosperous Nepal by investing in people: their education, health and voice, along with local livelihoods and appropriate technology.

Our work focuses on poor and underprivileged children and mountain communities in Humla and across Karnali, and on standing with communities when disaster strikes.

Our story

Sixteen years in the Himalaya

2008

READI is founded in Humla

A children's home opens in a rented building in Simikot.

2009

The first 20 children

Children from surrounding villages, selected for need regardless of caste or ethnicity, move into the hostel in Simikot.

2010

Humla's first library

Opened at the Simikot school with TRAS, the Nepal Library Foundation and Rato Bangala School. The Humla Delights café starts supporting the home.

2011

30 children, half of them girls

All doing well at school. Two teachers trained in modern teaching methods, and the hostel's cook uses locally grown, nutritious food.

2015

Earthquake relief

Relief for affected families in Sindhupalchowk, Rasuwa and Gorkha.

2016

Fighting child malnutrition

With DHO Humla, 111 health workers trained and 336 children with severe acute malnutrition identified.

2019

Partnership with Maresía ONG

School lunches and volunteer camps at Shree Krishna School, Changunarayan.

2020

COVID-19 response

Food for school families in Changunarayan in 2020, then oxygen and emergency health equipment for Humla in 2021.

2023

Jajarkot earthquake

Warm clothing for affected families as winter arrived.

2024

A permanent children's home

Opened in Simikot with support from the Government of Japan.

Transparency

Accountable to the people we serve, and to you

READI runs entirely on support from individuals and organizations around the world. Funds are managed by a small staff and a volunteer board of directors in Humla and Kathmandu, and administration costs are kept to a minimum.
