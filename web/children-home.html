Why it matters

Children in Humla shouldn't have to leave home to go to school

Humla is one of Nepal's most remote districts. For children from distant villages, from families facing poverty, or without family protection, getting a steady education can mean being sent far away.

The READI Children's Home lets them live safely in Simikot, attend school every day, and stay connected to their community.

Partners

Built with the People of Japan

The new building was funded by the Government of Japan's Grant Assistance for Grassroots Human Security Projects (USD 124,267). It was handed over on 11 December 2024 and also serves as a training centre for the community.

Day-to-day running costs have long been supported by the Trans-Himalayan Aid Society (TRAS) in Canada.

Who lives at the home?

The children most in need from villages around Simikot, selected regardless of caste, ethnicity or culture. Many have lost one or both parents, some during the decade-long armed conflict. They are housed and schooled in Simikot so they keep their own language, culture and traditions. By 2011 the home cared for 30 children, half of them girls.

Can I sponsor a specific child?

Sponsorships support the whole home rather than one child, so every child gets the same care. We'll send you updates from Simikot.

Can I visit or volunteer?

Yes, through our International Volunteer Program. Stays last 2 to 6 weeks, including teaching at Shree Balmandir School in Simikot.

How do I know my donation reaches the children?

We publish annual reports and audited accounts on our Transparency page.
